# Fact verification — The Magnet That Fell in Slow Motion

1. A magnet is slowed in a conducting, non-ferromagnetic copper tube.
2. Changing flux induces eddy currents; their fields oppose the change. Lower rings repel and upper rings attract a downward-moving magnet, both producing upward force.
3. At terminal speed, gravitational potential energy is dissipated as ohmic heating.
4. A longitudinal slit interrupts the large circumferential current paths and reduces braking; it does not eliminate all eddy currents or imply perfect free fall.

## Illustration limits

Transparent tube views are schematic X-rays, not a physical cut. The later slit is a narrow removed longitudinal strip. Circulation reverses above/below a north-up magnet; force arrows both point upward. Residual currents remain after slitting. Heating is represented by tiny energy pulses, without steam or glowing-hot copper. Fall speeds and scales illustrate the mechanism and are not experimental measurements. No scraping is shown.

An independent review found no material factual error in the 210-word narration.
